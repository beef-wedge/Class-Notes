## Core
##### Why is Refrigerant a Regulated Substance?
- Older refrigerants:
	- Such as R-22
	- Use chemicals such as **bromine** or **chlorine**
	- Bromine and chlorine deplete ozone molecules by breaking apart the O$_3$ to O$_2$ 
- Newer refrigerants:
	- Such as R-410A
	- Does not contain ozone depleting chemicals
	- R410A instead has a high global warming potential
##### The Clean Air Act
- Knowingly venting refrigerants is illegal
- Violators face daily fines exceeding $44,000 per violation.
##### The Three R’s:
- **Recover:** Removing refrigerant in any condition and storing it in an external container
- **Recycle:** Cleaning the refrigerant for reuse by running it through filter-driers to separate oil and moisture
- **Reclaim:** Processing refrigerant back to new product specifications (meeting AHRI 700 purity standards). It can only be resold once reclaimed
##### Safety & Shipping
- Never mix refrigerants in a recovery cylinder. Always use a scale to ensure cylinders aren't filled past 80% capacity.

## Small Appliances (Type I)
- This section covers factory-sealed, small systems containing **5 pounds or less** of refrigerant.
##### Examples
- Household refrigerators
- Freezers
- Window AC units
- Vending machines
##### Recovery Requirements
- If the system's compressor works, you must recover 90% of the refrigerant
- If the compressor is dead, you must recover 80%
##### Methods
- Passive (system-dependent) recovery uses the appliance's own compressor or pressure to push refrigerant out, while active (self-contained) recovery uses an independent recovery machine
---
## High Pressure Appliances (Type II)
- This section covers systems using medium to very high-pressure refrigerants.
##### Examples
- Residential split-system central air conditioners
- Heat pumps
- Supermarket walk-in coolers
- Common refrigerants include R-22 and R-410A
##### Leak Repair Requirements
- If a system contains **50 pounds or more** of refrigerant, owners must repair leaks if they exceed a certain annual rate:
    - Commercial Refrigeration: 20%
    - Industrial Process Refrigeration: 30%
    - Comfort Cooling (Residential/Office AC): 10%
##### Evacuation Levels
- Before performing major repairs, you must pull a deep vacuum using certified recovery equipment to specific levels (measured in inches of mercury, or Hg) to ensure no refrigerant escapes
---
## Low Pressure Appliances (Type I)
- This section focuses on massive industrial systems that operate in a vacuum (pressures below atmospheric pressure).
##### Examples
- Large commercial chillers using refrigerants like R-11 or R-123.
    
##### Unique Traits
- Because these systems operate below atmospheric pressure, leaks cause air and moisture to get _into_ the system rather than refrigerant getting out
##### Purge Units
- These systems use a purge unit to separate and vent the trapped air while returning the refrigerant back to the system.
##### Leak Thresholds
- The 50-pound leak thresholds are identical to Type II (10% comfort cooling, 20% commercial, 30% industrial), but leak detection requires pressurizing the system safely with hot water or nitrogen; never compress air into it
