## Section 4.1 - Detours and Midpoints
##### Detours
- Sometimes is it needed to prove more than 1 set of triangles congruent
- Procedure for Detour Proofs:
	1. Determine which triangles you must prove congruent to reach the required conclusion
	2. Attempt to prove those triangles congruent. If you cannot do so for lack of enough information, take a detour
	3. Identify the parts that you must prove to be congruent to establish the congruence of triangles
	4. Find a pair of triangles that
		- You can readily prove congruent
		- Contain a pair of parts needed for main proof
	5. Prove that the triangles found in step 4 are congruent
	6. Use CPCTC and complete the proof planned in step 1
##### Midpoint Formula
- On a number line (1D), If $A = (-2)$ and $B = 10$, the midpoint is $4$ (average)
- On a coordinate plane (2D), $\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2}$ is the midpoint
	- Average the X coordinate and Y coordinate seperately

## Section 4.3 - A Right Angle Theorem
##### A Right-Angle Theorem
- If two angles are both supplementary and congruent, then they form right angles
##### Distance
- The distance between 2 objects it the shortest possible path connecting them
- Postulate:
	- A line segment is the shortest path between 2 points

## Section 4.4 - Equidistance
##### Equidistance
- If two points *P* and *Q* are the same distance from a third point *X*, then *X* Is considered equidistant from *P* and *Q*
##### Perpendicular Bisector
- The perpendicular bisector of a segment is the line that bisects it and is perpendicular to the segment
##### A Perpendicular Bisector Theorem
- If 2 points are equidistant from end points of a segment, then the 2 points determine the perpendicular bisector of that segment

## Section 4.6 - Slope
##### Slope
- The slope of a non-vertical line, segment, or ray containing $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$
- Equations:
	- $\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$
	- Rise / Run (Rise over Run)
	- Change in $Y$ over Change in $X$
##### Slope of Parallel Lines
- If 2 non-vertical lines are parallel, then their slopes are the same
- If the slopes of non-vertical lines are the same, then the lines are parallel
##### Opposite Reciprocals
- Opposite reciprocals are fractions whose product is $-1$
##### Slope of Perpendicular Lines
- If two lines are perpendicular and neither is vertical, each line's slope is the opposite reciprocal of the other's
- If a line's slope is the opposite reciprocal of another line's slope the two lines are perpendicular
