## Section 5.1 - Indirect Proofs
##### Indirect Proof Procedure
1. List the possibilities for the conclusion
2. Assume the **negation** (opposite) of the desired conclusion is correct
3. Write a chain of reasons until you reach impossibility (contradic tion)
	- This will be a contradiction of either
		- Given information
		- A theorem, postulate or definition
4. State the remaining possibility of the desired conclusion

## Section 5.2 - Proving That Lines are Parallel
##### Exterior Angles
- The angle formed when one side of a triangle is extended to form an angle supplementary to the adjacent interior angle
- **Exterior Angle Inequality Theorem**: 
	- Exterior angle > Either remote interior angle
##### Parallel Lines
- Two co-planar (in the same plane) lines the do not intersect
##### Alternate Interior Angles Theorem
- If 2 lines are cut by a transversal such that 2 alternate interior angles are congruent, then the lines are parallel
##### Alternate Exterior Angles Theorem
- If 2 lines are cut by a transversal such that 2 alternate exterior angles are congruent, then the lines are parallel
##### Corresponding Angles Theorem
- If 2 lines are cut by a transversal such that 2 corresponding angles are congruent, then the lines are parallel
##### Consecutive Interior Angles Theorem
- If 2 lines are cut by a transversal such that 2 interior angles on the same side of the transversal are supplementary, then the lines are parallel
##### Consecutive Exterior Angles Theorem
- If 2 lines are cut by a transversal such that 2 exterior angles on the same side of the transversal and supplementary, then the lines are parallel
##### Another Perpendicular Theorem
- If 2 co-planar lines are perpendicular to a third line, then they are parallel

## Section 5.3 - Congruent Angles Associated with Parallel Lines
##### Parallel Postulate
- Through a point not on a line, there is exactly one parallel to the given line

##### More Parallel and Perpendicular Theorems
- In a plane, if a line is perpendicular to 1 of 2 parallel lines, it is perpendicular to the other
- If 2 lines are parallel to a third line, they are all parallel to eachother (by transitive property)

## Section 5.4
##### Polygon
- Plane figures (flat) which must be closed and that do not include curves
- Examples:
	- Triangle
	- Square
	- Kite
	- Rhombus
	- N-Gon
##### Naming Polygons
1. Start at any vertex
2. Proceed clockwise or counterclockwise (do not skip around)
##### Convex or Concave
- Convex: When each interior angle measures more that 180 degrees
- Otherwise, the polygon is concave
##### Diagonals
- Any segment that connects two non-adjacent vertices of any given polygon
##### Quadrilateral
- A 4-sided polygon
- Examples:
	- Parallelogram
	- Kite
	- Trapezoid
	- Square
	- Rectangle
	- Rhombus

## Section 5.5
##### Parallelograms
- Opposite pairs of sides are parallel and congruent by definition
- Opposite pairs of angles (across diagonals) are congruent
- Diagonals bisect eachother at their intersection
- Any pair of consecutive interior (directly adjacent) angles are supplementary
##### Kites
- 2 disjoint pairs of consecutive side
- Each pair of consecutive sides are congruent
- One diagonal perpendicularly bisects the other
- Lower base angle supplementary to any other base angle
##### Rectangles
- Has all properties of parallelogram by definition
- All angles are right angles
- Diagonals are congruent
##### Rhombus (Rhombi)
- All properties of kites and parallelogram by definition
- Equilateral (all sides congruent)
- Diagonals bisect all angles
-  Diagonals perpendicularly bisect eachother
##### Square (Regular quadrilateral)
- All properties of rectangle, rhombus and kite by definition
##### Isosceles Trapezoids
- Not a parallelogram by definition
- Base segments are parallel
- Lower base angles are congruent
- Upper base angles are congruent
- Diagonals are congruent

## Section 5.6
##### Ways to prove a quadrilateral is a parallelogram
- Both pairs of opposite sides are congruent
- Single pair of opp sides parallel and congruent
- Diagonals bisect
- Both pairs of opposite angles are congruent

## Section 5.7
### Proving that a quadrilateral is a...
##### Rectangle
- Prove the quadrilateral is a parallelogram
	- If the parallelogram contains at least one ___ , then the parallelogram is a rectangle
	- If the parallelogram contains ___ , then the parallelogram is a rectangle
- Without proving it is a parallelogram:
	- If ___ of a quadrilateral ___ , then the quadrilateral is a rectangle
##### Kite
- If 2 disjoint consecutive sides of a quadrilateral are congruent, then a quadrilateral is a kite
- If one of the diagonals of a quadrilateral is the perpendicular bisector of the other diagonal, then the quadrilateral is a kite
##### Rhombus
- Prove the quadrilateral is a Parallelogram, then..
	- If a parallelogram contains consecutive sides that are congruent, then the parallelogram is a rhombus
	- If either diagonal of a parallelogram bisects 2 angles, then the parallelogram is a rhombus
- Without proving its a parallelogram
	- If both diagonals are perpendicular bisectors of the other, then the quadrilateral is a rhombus
##### Square
- Prove rectangle and rhombus
##### Isosceles Trapezoid
- Prove that one side pair is parallel, then..
		- If one pair of opposite sides of a quadrilateral are congruent, then it is an isosceles trapezoid
	- If lower or upper base angles are congruent, then it is an isosceles trapezoid
	- If the diagonals are congruent, then it is an isosceles trapezoid
