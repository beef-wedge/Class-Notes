## Section T.1 - Reflections
##### Transformation
- An operation that maps an original figure called the pre-image onto a new figure called the image
- A transformation can change the size, position or orientation of a figure
##### Reflection
- A flip over a line called the line of reflection
- Each point and its image are the same distance from the line of reflection
- Possible lines of reflection:
	- X-axis or Y-axis
	- Any vertical or horizontal line
	- Diagonal lines in the form $y=x$ or $y=-x$
---
## Section T.2 - Translations
##### Translations
- To vertically and/or horizontally slide a figure
- Symbolic Form:
	- Function Notation: $(x,y)\to(x+h,y+k)$
	- Vector notation $<h,k>$
	- $h$ represents the horizontal shift
	- $k$ represents the vertical shift
---
## Section T.3 - Rotations
##### Rotations
- A rotation around a fixed point called the center of rotation
- The figure rotates at a specific angle and direction
- Rotation shortcut (counterclockwise, around the origin)
	- 90 degrees: $(x,y)\to(-y,x)$
	- 180 degrees: $(x,y)\to(-x,-y)$
	- 270 degrees: $(x,y)\to(y,-x)$
---
## Section T.5 - Symmetry
##### Symmetry
- A figure has symmetry if there exists a rigid motion (reflections, translations or rotations) that maps the figure onto itself
##### Line of Symmetry
- A figure has symmetry if the figure can be mapped onto itself by a reflection in a line called a line of symmetry
##### Rotational Symmetry
- A figure has rotational symmetry if the figure can be mapped onto itself by a rotation between 0 and 360 degrees about the center of rotation
##### Order and Magnitude
- Order is the number a figure maps onto itself from 0 to 360 degrees
- Magnitude of symmetry (or angle of rotation) is the smallest angle a figure can be rotated so that it maps onto itself
- Formula: $m=\frac{360}{o}$ where $m$ is magnitude and $o$ is order
##### Plane of Symmetry
- A three-dimensional figure has a plane of symmetry if the figure can be mapped onto itself by a reflection in a plane
##### Axis of Symmetry
- A three-dimensional figure has an axis of symmetry if the figure can be mapped onto itself by a rotation between 0 and 360 degrees

## Section T.6 - Dilations
##### Dilations
- The enlargement or reduction of a figure
- The scale factor  indicated how much the factor will enlarge or reduce
- The variable for scale factor is $k$
	- When $k>1$, the dilation is an enlargement
	- When $k<1$, the dilation is a reduction
##### Transformations: Similar vs. Congruent
- Dilations produce similar figures
- Rigid motions (translations, reflections, and rotations) produce congruent figures
##### Dilations in a Coordinate Plane
- Given by the function notation: $(x,y)\to(ky,ky)$
